## PURPOSE OF THIS SET (PLAIN ENGLISH)

Claims 28–40 protect the *structures* of the platform: the Model Context Protocol (MCP) discovery
layer, the hierarchical agentic workforce, the white-label enterprise tenancy, the privileged-column
security framework, and the economic circulation graph.

Claims 41–45 protect the five *execution paths* a competitor would most plausibly use to build an
equivalent system while arguing it falls outside Claims 28–40:

1. Letting an agent complete a high-value action without hardware-bound human approval (Claim 41).
2. Using two or more different models to cross-check extracted data instead of one (Claim 42).
3. Placing a sanitizing proxy in front of the protocol endpoint instead of hardening the tools (Claim 43).
4. Recomputing the circulation multiplier from live local telemetry instead of using a fixed constant (Claim 44).
5. Carrying attribution through a live voice channel instead of a click or scan (Claim 45).

Numbering continues the original 27 claims and Claims 28–40 so all three sets merge into a single
non-provisional utility application without renumbering.

## CLAIM 41: HARDWARE-BOUND HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP STEP-UP FOR HIGH-VALUE AUTONOMOUS AGENT ACTIONS

### Independent Claim 41

A computer-implemented security method for gating the completion of autonomous software-agent tasks
that carry financial or legal consequence, comprising:

a) receiving, at an orchestration layer, a proposed terminal action emitted by an autonomous agent
of the hierarchical agentic workforce of Claim 30, said proposed action comprising a target
persistence operation, one or more affected records, and a machine-computed consequence magnitude;

b) evaluating the consequence magnitude against a configurable, per-division and per-agent
step-up threshold expressed as at least one of a monetary amount, a count of affected principals, a
privileged-column classification, or an irreversibility flag;

c) responsive to the threshold being met or exceeded, suspending the proposed action in a pending
state, persisting an immutable pre-image of the affected records, and refusing to commit the
underlying database transaction;

d) transmitting an out-of-band step-up challenge to a registered human principal over a channel
distinct from the channel on which the agent operates;

e) requiring, as a condition of release, an attestation generated by a hardware-backed key store
resident on the principal's device — including a trusted execution environment, secure enclave,
platform authenticator, or equivalent — the attestation binding (i) a cryptographic digest of the
proposed action payload, (ii) a server-issued single-use nonce, and (iii) the device credential
identifier;

f) verifying the attestation signature server-side against the principal's previously enrolled
public credential, verifying nonce freshness within a bounded validity window, and verifying that
the digest matches the suspended payload byte-for-byte, thereby preventing substitution of the
action between approval and commit;

g) committing the suspended action within a single atomic database transaction only upon successful
verification, and otherwise expiring the pending action and emitting a rejection event; and

h) writing a tamper-evident approval record comprising the principal identity, credential
identifier, attestation result, timestamp, and payload digest to an append-only audit ledger keyed
to the idempotent agent run log of Claim 30.

### Dependent Claim 41.1

The method of Claim 41, wherein the step-up threshold is dynamically lowered for an agent whose
recent output has been negatively rated through the closed learning loop of Claim 30.1.

### Dependent Claim 41.2

The method of Claim 41, wherein the affected records include any column enumerated in the
privileged-column registry of Claim 34, such that the hardware step-up and the before-update
privileged-column trigger operate as independent, serially-enforced controls.

### Dependent Claim 41.3

The method of Claim 41, wherein the out-of-band channel is selected by policy from push
notification to a registered native client, short message service, or electronic mail containing a
single-use deep link, and wherein the selected channel is recorded in the approval record.

### Dependent Claim 41.4

The method of Claim 41, wherein a plurality of distinct human principals must independently attest
when the consequence magnitude exceeds a second, higher threshold, and wherein the required quorum
is stored per enterprise organization under the tenancy model of Claim 32.

### Dependent Claim 41.5

The method of Claim 41, wherein an unverified, expired, or repeatedly failed step-up automatically
suspends the emitting agent's tool permissions pending administrative review.

## CLAIM 42: MULTI-MODEL CONSENSUS AND ANTI-HALLUCINATION PIPELINE FOR AUTONOMOUS RECORD ENRICHMENT

### Independent Claim 42

A computer-implemented method for establishing machine-verifiable confidence in entity attributes
extracted from unstructured public sources, comprising:

a) retrieving a corpus of public web artifacts associated with a candidate directory record;

b) submitting the corpus to a first language model under a constrained output schema to produce a
first candidate attribute set comprising at least an owner or principal name, a contact endpoint,
and a source citation for each extracted attribute;

c) submitting the same corpus, under an equivalent constrained output schema and an independently
composed instruction, to at least one second language model that is architecturally distinct from
the first model with respect to at least one of model family, training provenance, or serving
vendor;

d) computing a cross-model agreement index by normalizing each attribute of the first and second
candidate sets to a canonical form and applying a per-attribute-type comparison function, wherein
the comparison function for name-typed attributes tolerates ordering and honorific variance and the
comparison function for contact-typed attributes requires exact canonical equality;

e) computing a composite confidence score from the cross-model agreement index weighted by source
authority of the cited artifact and by attribute-level extraction certainty;

f) automatically accepting the attribute set into an administrator-only private table joined by
foreign key to the public record when the composite confidence score meets a configured acceptance
threshold, and otherwise enqueuing the record, both candidate sets, and the disagreement vector
into a human evaluation queue; and

g) withholding every extracted personally identifiable attribute from all public application
programming interfaces and from the protocol tool surface of Claim 28 regardless of confidence
score.

### Dependent Claim 42.1

The method of Claim 42, wherein a third model arbitrates attribute-level disagreements between the
first and second models, and wherein arbitration outcomes are retained as labeled training evidence
for threshold recalibration.

### Dependent Claim 42.2

The method of Claim 42, wherein human accept/reject decisions taken from the evaluation queue
adjust the acceptance threshold and the per-model authority weights over time.

### Dependent Claim 42.3

The method of Claim 42, wherein an attribute asserted by exactly one model and contradicted by no
model is retained as provisional and is expressly excluded from any outbound communication until
independently corroborated.

### Dependent Claim 42.4

The method of Claim 42, wherein the agreement index, model identifiers, model versions, and prompt
digests are persisted per record, producing a reproducible provenance trail for each enriched
attribute.

### Dependent Claim 42.5

The method of Claim 42, wherein enrichment is performed by a designated agent of the workforce of
Claim 30 whose tool permissions permit writes solely to the private enrichment table through a
security-definer function with a locked search path.

## CLAIM 43: DYNAMIC PROMPT AND SCHEMA SANITIZATION LAYER FOR UNCURATED AGENT INVOCATIONS

### Independent Claim 43

A zero-trust request-mediation system interposed between third-party artificial-intelligence
assistants and a protocol-exposed data tier, comprising:

a) an ingress proxy terminating protocol tool invocations directed at the Model Context Protocol
server of Claim 28 before any database statement is planned or executed;

b) a strict schema validator that rejects any invocation whose parameters fail exact conformance to
the tool's declared JSON schema, including rejection on unknown properties, out-of-range numerics,
oversized string payloads, and disallowed types, wherein rejection occurs prior to authorization
evaluation;

c) an instruction-isolation filter that scans free-text parameter values for system-prompt override
patterns, role-reassignment directives, tool-enumeration probes, exfiltration directives, and
encoded or homoglyph-obfuscated variants thereof, and that neutralizes such content by escaping it
into an inert, non-instructional data frame rather than forwarding it as model-interpretable text;

d) a context-frame constructor that server-side injects the tenant identifier, principal identifier,
and authorization tier resolved from the verified bearer token of Claim 28 into the execution
context, wherein said identifiers are never sourced from, and cannot be overridden by, any
client-supplied parameter;

e) an egress filter that inspects each response prior to transmission and redacts any field not
present in the tool's declared response schema, including any personally identifiable attribute
governed by Claim 42(g); and

f) an anomaly ledger recording rejected invocations, the triggering rule, and the calling agent
client identifier of Claim 29, wherein a client exceeding a configured rejection rate is
automatically rate-limited or quarantined.

### Dependent Claim 43.1

The system of Claim 43, wherein the injected tenant identifier is bound to the database session
such that row-level-security predicates evaluate against the server-resolved principal, rendering
parameter-level tenant spoofing structurally impossible.

### Dependent Claim 43.2

The system of Claim 43, wherein the instruction-isolation filter additionally scans stored record
content returned from the database, defending against second-order injection in which malicious
instructions were previously persisted by a directory registrant.

### Dependent Claim 43.3

The system of Claim 43, wherein the anomaly ledger feeds the autonomous security autopilot of
Claim 35, which may auto-quarantine an agent client and emit a remediation digest entry.

### Dependent Claim 43.4

The system of Claim 43, wherein every read tool is executed under a database role possessing no
write privilege whatsoever, such that a successful injection cannot mutate state.

### Dependent Claim 43.5

The system of Claim 43, wherein the proxy enforces per-tool, per-client, and per-tenant invocation
ceilings evaluated within a sliding window, and returns a standardized retry directive on breach.

## CLAIM 44: ADAPTIVE ECONOMIC MULTIPLIER CALIBRATION VIA LOCAL CIRCULATION TELEMETRY

### Independent Claim 44

A computer-implemented system for dynamically calibrating a community wealth-circulation multiplier
from measured local economic telemetry rather than a static baseline constant, comprising:

a) continuously ingesting attributed transaction events from the circulation graph of Claim 40, each
event carrying a geographic locus, a merchant node, a member node, an amount, and an origination
channel;

b) partitioning the event stream into calibration cells defined by at least one of postal code,
metropolitan statistical area, or enterprise chapter under the tenancy hierarchy of Claim 32;

c) computing, per calibration cell and per rolling observation window, a local circulation velocity
statistic derived from the ratio of intra-cell merchant-to-merchant and member-to-merchant value
flow to value exiting the cell, together with a dwell measure of median time between successive
in-network transactions of the same currency unit;

d) deriving a cell-specific circulation multiplier by adjusting a global baseline multiplier toward
the measured local velocity statistic, subject to a shrinkage function that weights the local
estimate by observed sample sufficiency and reverts to the baseline where sample volume is
insufficient;

e) clamping each derived multiplier within configured floor and ceiling bounds and rate-limiting
period-over-period movement to prevent oscillation or manipulation;

f) applying the cell-specific multiplier in place of the baseline when computing sponsor impact,
member impact, and chapter revenue-share reporting under Claims 2 and 33; and

g) persisting each calibration epoch with its inputs, resulting multiplier, and sample counts, such
that any published impact figure is reproducible and auditable to its underlying telemetry.

### Dependent Claim 44.1

The system of Claim 44, wherein calibration cells exhibiting anomalous velocity consistent with
wash-trading are excluded and referred to the geospatial velocity fraud detection of Claim 4.

### Dependent Claim 44.2

The system of Claim 44, wherein the cell-specific multiplier is exposed to an autonomous agent of
Claim 30 that recommends allocation of sponsor or grant capital toward cells with the highest
marginal circulation response.

### Dependent Claim 44.3

The system of Claim 44, wherein each reported impact figure is accompanied by a confidence interval
derived from the sample sufficiency weighting of step (d).

### Dependent Claim 44.4

The system of Claim 44, wherein the baseline multiplier remains the disclosed default value and the
calibrated value is presented as an adjusted figure with its epoch identifier.

### Dependent Claim 44.5

The system of Claim 44, wherein multipliers are computed separately for agent-originated commerce
under Claim 29 and human-originated commerce, quantifying the incremental circulation attributable
to the protocol discovery layer.

## CLAIM 45: CROSS-MODAL CONVERSATIONAL CITATION AND ATTRIBUTION ENGINE

### Independent Claim 45

A method for preserving a commerce attribution chain across a real-time spoken-audio interaction and
into a subsequent digital or physical transaction, comprising:

a) establishing a bidirectional low-latency audio session between a human user and the conversational
voice system of Claims 6 and 11 over a persistent socket transport;

b) minting, at session establishment, a temporal session token bound to the authenticated or
anonymous principal, the session start time, the originating surface, and, where applicable, the
referring agent client identifier of Claim 29;

c) responsive to the voice system naming a verified directory business during the session, recording
a spoken-referral event associating the temporal session token, the business identifier, the
utterance timestamp, and a normalized transcript span constituting the referral;

d) emitting to the user, over a companion channel, a machine-readable continuation artifact carrying
the temporal session token, comprising at least one of a deep link, a scannable code, or a
short-lived claim identifier;

e) matching a subsequent event — comprising a profile view, a check-in of the atomic scan pipeline of
Claim 9, a loyalty award, or a completed transaction — to the spoken-referral event by (i) direct
presentation of the continuation artifact, or (ii) where no artifact is presented, a probabilistic
join over principal identity, business identity, and a bounded attribution window, the join yielding
an attribution confidence score;

f) persisting the resulting attribution chain from voice utterance through terminal transaction, and
attributing the transaction to the voice modality for revenue-share settlement under Claims 29 and
33; and

g) requiring, before any business may be named as a referral in step (c), that the business hold an
active verification badge under the propagation state machine of Claim 39, and emitting the
canonical citation of Claim 28.2 with the spoken referral.

### Dependent Claim 45.1

The method of Claim 45, wherein the attribution window and confidence weighting are configured per
enterprise organization under Claim 32.

### Dependent Claim 45.2

The method of Claim 45, wherein revocation of a business's verification badge invalidates
outstanding continuation artifacts referencing that business.

### Dependent Claim 45.3

The method of Claim 45, wherein spoken referrals and their downstream conversions are aggregated into
the circulation graph of Claim 40 as edges labeled with the voice modality.

### Dependent Claim 45.4

The method of Claim 45, wherein the normalized transcript span is retained as evidentiary support for
the attribution and is subject to a configured retention period and principal-initiated deletion.

### Dependent Claim 45.5

The method of Claim 45, wherein a single temporal session token supports a plurality of spoken
referrals to distinct businesses, and attribution among them is apportioned by recency and by
explicit user acknowledgement captured within the audio session.
